# Opening a Small Café (Under 30 Seats): Bengaluru vs. Singapore vs. Dubai

## Executive Summary

This report compares the complete regulatory pathway for a founder opening a small café (food and beverage outlet, under 30 seats) in three jurisdictions. The analysis is based exclusively on official government sources retrieved as of 11 November 2026. **Singapore offers the clearest, lowest-cost, fastest, and most digitally streamlined process.** Bengaluru has the lowest headline government fees but the highest regulatory opacity—critical information on BBMP trade licence fees and Fire NOC fees for cafés is not reliably documented in official sources. Dubai has the most complex multi-agency process and the highest fees, but also the most foreign-founder-friendly ownership rules.

**Critical disclosure:** For Dubai, the DET/DED official portal does not publish a specific fee schedule for restaurant/café trade licences on its public pages [9][17][31]. For Bengaluru, BBMP does not publish an accessible official fee schedule online for small cafés. Where no authoritative official figure exists, this report writes **UNCLEAR** rather than guessing.

## Section 1: Bengaluru, India

### Context

A small café in Bengaluru operates under a layered federal–state–municipal regulatory system. The central food safety regulator (FSSAI) is the most clearly documented layer. The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) trade licence, Karnataka State Fire & Emergency Services NOC, Karnataka Professional Tax, and GST registration each have their own portals and processes.

**Important update (effective 1 April 2026):** FSSAI introduced perpetual validity of licences/registrations—FBOs are no longer required to renew FSSAI licences annually; they remain valid unless suspended, cancelled, or surrendered. Fees may still be paid for any number of years at once [29].

## Section 2: Singapore

### Context

Singapore operates a highly digitised, single-window licensing environment through GoBusiness. For a small café under 30 seats, the primary regulatory bodies are ACRA (business registration), SFA (food shop licence), URA (land use/change of use), and CPF/IRAS (tax obligations). The process is well-documented in official sources.

**Important note on Fire Certificate:** SCDF's Fire Certificate is required for public buildings with occupant load **over 200 persons** [24]. A café under 30 seats almost certainly does not trigger this threshold. It is therefore not listed as a mandatory step, though SCDF fire safety compliance (extinguishers, smoke detectors, etc.) must still be maintained.

## Section 3: Dubai, UAE

### Context

Opening a café in Dubai (mainland) requires coordination between at least three government bodies: the **Dubai Department of Economy and Tourism (DET)** for the trade licence, **Dubai Municipality (Food Safety Department)** for the food establishment permit, and potentially **Dubai Civil Defence** for fire safety compliance. The DET/DED portal exists [9][17][31] but does not publish a downloadable, specific fee schedule for restaurant/café trade licences on its public-facing pages. Official fee figures for the trade licence are therefore **UNCLEAR** from the sources retrieved. Dubai Municipality publishes some specific permit fees [26] and food establishment activity requirements [8][27], but not the fee for the main food establishment licence.

**Free Zone vs. Mainland note:** This analysis covers Dubai mainland (DET-licensed). Free zone options exist (e.g., DMCC, Dubai South) with different rules; free zone businesses generally cannot serve walk-in public directly without a mainland branch. That pathway is excluded here.

## Recommendation: Easiest Place to Start

### Recommended: **Singapore**

Singapore is the clearest, most transparently documented, and fastest jurisdiction for a founder opening a small café.

**Reasons:**

1. **Full fee transparency:** Every government fee is published on official portals. The SFA Food Shop Licence costs exactly SGD $195/year [3][6], ACRA registration is $115 for a one-year sole proprietorship [5], and the URA change of use application (if needed) is $500 [25]. There are no hidden or unclear mandatory government fees for the core licences.

2. **Fastest confirmed pathway:** SFA reviews completed applications within 7 working days [3]. ACRA registration is typically same-day. The total regulatory clock, excluding renovation time, is approximately 2–4 weeks for a well-prepared applicant.

3. **Single digital window:** GoBusiness consolidates all licence applications, food handler registration, and renewal in one portal. The GoBusiness Licence e-Adviser helps identify all required licences [18].

4. **No mandatory annual renewal of food licence admin complexity:** The SFA renewal is a simple online payment of $195, effective immediately [11].

5. **Perpetual SAFE grade framework:** From January 2026, the SAFE framework [12] grades establishments transparently, reducing uncertainty about ongoing compliance obligations.

6. **Lowest confirmed total government fee** among the three jurisdictions for the core mandatory licences (approximately SGD $310–$815 depending on structure and whether URA change of use is needed).

## Trade-offs of Choosing Singapore

| Trade-off | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Higher operating costs overall** | Singapore has among the world's highest rents, labour costs, and cost of living. Government fees are low, but total cost of business is high. |
| **Foreign founder residency requirement** | If the founder is not a Singapore Citizen, Permanent Resident, or eligible EP holder, they must appoint a local resident director for a private limited company, or use a sole proprietorship structure (which requires the owner to be a resident) [5][4]. An Employment Pass or EntrePass may be required for the founder to work in Singapore — this is a separate MOM process not covered in this report. |
| **Strict food safety enforcement** | Singapore's demerit point system and the new SAFE framework [12] mean licences can be suspended or revoked for accumulated violations. Category 2 establishments (kitchen under 16m²) have no fast-track to 'A' grade [12]. |
| **Small market size** | Singapore's population is ~6 million. Bengaluru (13M+) and Dubai (3.5M+ with high tourist and expat traffic) offer different scale opportunities. |
| **URA zoning restrictions** | Several road stretches in Singapore are explicitly listed as unlikely to approve new restaurant use [13]. Thorough location due diligence is required before signing a lease. |
| **Stamp Duty on lease** | Tenancy agreements must be stamped at IRAS; this cost (which varies with lease duration and rent quantum) is a prerequisite for the SFA licence application [3] but cannot be quantified without specific lease terms. |
| **NEA Environmental Sanitation Programme** | Selected coffee shops and eating houses must submit an Environmental Sanitation Programme to NEA within 1 month of opening [4][6]. Whether a small café is "selected" is determined by NEA; this adds an operational compliance step. |

## Methodology and Confidence Notes

- **All fees and timelines cited are from official government sources retrieved on 11 November 2026**, as identified by the evidence brief. Sources are cited inline throughout using [N] notation.
- **Where official sources do not state a fee, processing time, or requirement, this report writes UNCLEAR.** No estimates from third-party or consultancy sources have been treated as confirmed facts. Low-quality third-party estimates for Dubai (from [19], [32], [33]) are referenced only as contextual estimates and are explicitly labelled as unconfirmed.
- **FSSAI perpetual validity (India):** As of 1 April 2026, FSSAI licences and registrations have perpetual validity and do not require annual renewal [29]. This is a material change from prior years and reduces the annual compliance burden in Bengaluru.
- **Dubai DET fee opacity:** The official DET portal and Invest in Dubai portal do not publish specific trade licence fees for restaurant activities on their public-facing pages [9][17][31]. This is a known limitation of Dubai's regulatory transparency for new entrants; fees are typically quoted case-by-case or through the DET service counters.
- **This report covers government fees only.** It excludes: lease/rent costs, fit-out costs, equipment, staffing, marketing, insurance, food safety course provider fees (non-government), and professional/legal fees.
- Founders should verify all requirements directly with the relevant authorities before committing to a location, as regulations and fees can change between the retrieval date and the time of application.
